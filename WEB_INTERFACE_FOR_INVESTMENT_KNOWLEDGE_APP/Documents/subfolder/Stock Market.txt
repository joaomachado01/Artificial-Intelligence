The stock market is an environment where investors buy and sell shares of publicly traded companies. Here is an overview of how the stock market works and its main elements:

What are stocks?
Stocks represent small portions of a company's capital. When you buy a share, you are acquiring a part of the company and becoming a shareholder. This entitles you to a share of the profits (dividends) and, in some cases, voting rights at shareholder meetings.

How does the stock market work?
The stock market operates through stock exchanges, such as the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) or B3 in Brazil. On these exchanges, shares are bought and sold between investors. The price of shares is determined by supply and demand: when more people want to buy a share, the price rises; when more people want to sell, the price falls.

Types of shares
Common Shares (ON): Grant voting rights at shareholder meetings and a share of the company's profits.
Preferred Shares (PN): Generally do not grant voting rights, but have priority in receiving dividends and in the repayment of capital if the company is liquidated.
Market participants
Individual Investors: Private individuals who buy and sell shares to achieve their financial goals.
Institutional Investors: Entities such as pension funds, mutual funds and insurance companies that buy and sell shares on a large scale.
Brokerage Firms: Companies that facilitate the buying and selling of shares, offering trading platforms and advisory services.
Regulators: Organizations such as the Securities and Exchange Commission of Brazil (CVM), which supervise and regulate the market to protect investors and maintain market integrity.
Why invest in stocks?
Return Potential: Historically, stocks have delivered higher returns than other types of investments, such as bonds and savings accounts.
Profit Sharing: Through dividends, shareholders receive a portion of the company's profits.
Diversification: Investing in stocks of different companies and sectors can help diversify a portfolio and reduce risk.
Stock market risks
Volatility: Stock prices can fluctuate significantly over short periods of time due to various economic, political and company-specific factors.
Market Risk: The possibility of losing part or all of the investment due to falling stock prices.
Liquidity Risk: The difficulty of selling a share quickly without affecting its market price.
Investment Analysis
Fundamental Analysis: Assesses a company's financial health and prospects based on financial indicators, historical performance, quality of management and growth potential.
Technical Analysis: Studies price movements and trading volumes in the market to identify patterns and forecast future stock movements.
Final Considerations
Investing in the stock market can be an excellent way to build wealth over time, but it is important to understand the risks and carry out careful analysis before making investment decisions. Strategies such as diversification, long-term investing and consulting finance professionals can help mitigate risks and increase the chances of success.
